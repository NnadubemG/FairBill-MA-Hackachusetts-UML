# Product

## Product description

The Massachusetts Medical-Bill Helper is a web app that helps Massachusetts residents make
sense of confusing medical bills. A user uploads or pastes a bill (or an Explanation of
Benefits), and the app extracts the line items, explains common charges and billing codes in
plain language, flags things worth questioning (duplicate charges, balance billing, amounts
that look inconsistent with an EOB), points to the relevant Massachusetts patient-protection
resources and consumer programs, and helps draft a polite letter to a provider or insurer to
ask a question, request an itemized bill, or dispute a charge. The goal is to reduce confusion
and help people take their own next step with confidence.

## User framing note

Write for a stressed, non-expert person who may be worried about money and health. Assume no
knowledge of insurance jargon or billing codes. Be calm, clear, and plain-spoken. Explain
terms the first time they appear. Keep the user in control: the app informs and drafts, the
user decides and acts. Never pressure, alarm, or promise outcomes. Favor short sentences and
concrete next steps over dense explanation. Respect that the user's situation is sensitive and
personal; stay neutral and supportive, never judgmental.

## Guidance only, not legal or medical advice

This app provides general information and guidance only. It is **not legal advice and not
medical advice**, and using it does not create any professional relationship. It cannot
determine your rights, diagnose a health condition, or tell you what a bill legally owes or
does not owe. Information may be incomplete or not applicable to your specific situation. For
decisions about your health, consult a licensed medical professional. For decisions about your
rights, a dispute, or anything involving money you may owe, consult a qualified professional or
a Massachusetts consumer-assistance program. Always verify important information with your
provider, your insurer, or an official Massachusetts source before acting.

## Banned words

The UI, generated text, drafted letters, and model-facing copy must **never** use the
following words or phrases. They imply professional advice, legal conclusions, accusations, or
guaranteed outcomes that this app cannot and must not make. When a banned term is the obvious
word, use the suggested neutral alternative instead.

| Banned word / phrase | Why it's banned | Use instead |
|----------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| guarantee / guaranteed | Promises an outcome the app cannot ensure | "may help", "could", "often" |
| ensure (an outcome) | Same as guarantee | "may", "can help you" |
| promise | Promises an outcome | "aims to", "can help" |
| legal advice | Implies professional legal counsel | "general information" |
| medical advice | Implies professional medical counsel | "general information" |
| diagnose / diagnosis | Implies a clinical judgment | "general information about" |
| lawsuit | Legal conclusion / escalation the app can't advise | "dispute", "formal complaint" |
| sue / suing | Legal action the app can't advise | "file a complaint", "dispute" |
| fraud / fraudulent | Accusation / legal conclusion | "possible billing error", "charge worth questioning" |
| scam | Accusation | "charge worth questioning" |
| illegal / unlawful | Legal conclusion | "may not be allowed — worth confirming" |
| liable / liability | Legal conclusion about responsibility | "may be responsible — confirm" |
| entitled (to) | Asserts a legal right | "may be eligible — check" |
| rights (as a legal claim) | Asserts legal rights | "protections that may apply" |
| attorney / lawyer (as a recommendation to retain one) | Professional referral the app shouldn't make | "a qualified professional" |
| certainly / definitely / always / never (as outcome claims) | Overstates certainty | "often", "typically", "in many cases" |
| win / beat (the bill/insurer) | Promises an adversarial outcome | "resolve", "get answers" |
| must (you must pay / you must not pay) | Dictates a legal/financial obligation | "you may want to", "consider" |
| owe / don't owe (as a determination) | States a financial/legal conclusion | "the bill shows" / "worth confirming what you owe" |
| penalty-free / risk-free | Promises no consequences | "a common next step is" |

### Rules for using this list
- Match case-insensitively and catch obvious variants (plurals, verb tenses: sue/sued/suing,
  diagnose/diagnosed/diagnosing).
- The ban applies to app-authored and model-generated text. It does **not** require censoring a
  user's own words echoed back for confirmation, but the app should not adopt them as its own
  conclusions.
- When unsure, choose the softer, non-conclusive phrasing. The app informs and drafts; it never
  concludes, accuses, or promises.
